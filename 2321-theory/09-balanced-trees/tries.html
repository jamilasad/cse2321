The search box that remembers

Type two letters and the box already knows what you mean. Behind it is a tree of letters, a trie. Searching it costs the length of the word, not the number of words.

    Section 2

    One letter per edge

    ⏱ 7 min

    The words

    A trie is a tree that stores words letter by letter. Each edge is one letter. Each node stands for one beginning, the letters on the path down to it. The root is the empty beginning.

    Trees 1 called the root "start". Here it takes the code's name, root.

    Your turn

    The five names have 25 letters between them. How many letter nodes does their trie need?

    13, plus the root. A beginning that several names share is stored once: a serves all five, ab serves four.

    Section 3

    A name inside a name

    ⏱ 5 min

    In Trees 1's trie every word ended at a leaf. In ours, abdul's last l has a child of its own, because abdullah goes on.

    Your turn

    The walks for abdu and for abdul both stop on a node that has children. abdul is a name and abdu is not. How can the trie tell them apart?

    It puts a mark on the node where a name ends. abdul's l carries it; abdu's u does not. The drawings show the mark as an amber ring with a small clock, as a search box marks a past search. In the code it is called end.

    Section 4

    The node in C++

    ⏱ 8 min

    A node must find its child for any letter. There are 26 letters. Segment 1 gave you c - 'a', which turns a letter into a number from 0 to 25.

    Your turn

    How can a node find its d child in one step?

    It keeps an array of 26 child pointers. child['d' - 'a'], which is child[3], is the d child. A child that is not there is nullptr. One bool end holds the mark.

    Which slot is a letter's?
    
  
    Pick a letter. The slot it lights is the one its child pointer lives in.
    
  
    
    
    
    
    
    
    
  
    

  
    
    
    The letter is not in the node
    
    
    A node stores no letter at all. Its letter is which slot of its parent points to it. The drawings write the letter in the circle only to save room.

      Dig deeper · other alphabets
      
    
        
    The hero's course codes use letters, digits and a hyphen. A node for those needs 37 slots, or 128 for every ASCII character.
    
        
    A map<char, Node*> keeps only the children that exist, at the cost of a slower lookup.

    The two ways a search fails

    Section 8
    What it costs, and where it runs
    
    ⏱ 6 min
  
    

  
    
    
    Your turn
    
    
    50,000 names are stored. How many steps does search("abdullah") take? And with only our 5 names?
    
    
    
    8 both times: one step per letter. The number of names never enters the walk.
    
  
    

  
    
    
    ExplorerSearching abdullah among n names
    
    
    
      
    
        
    
        
    
      
    
      
    i
    
    
    
  
    

  
    A sorted array can answer a prefix query too: binary search finds where the abd names start, in about L · log n. The trie needs no search at all.
    

  
    
    
    TimeO(L)
    One step per letter of the word searched. n, the number of words, is not in it.
    
    
    Space216 bytes
    sizeof(Node): 26 pointers of 8 bytes, and the flag padded to 8. The five names take 14 nodes, 3,024 bytes, for 25 letters. Of their 364 links, 351 are null.
    
  
    

  
    Where it runs
    
  
    
    
    
    
  
    
  
    
    
    
      
    
      
    
      
    
    
    
    
    The three chips are the first three names below what you typed, a to z.
    
  
    
  
    
    
    
      
    
      
    
    
    
  
    
  
    
    
    The hero's box. Its alphabet is letters, digits and a hyphen, so its nodes are bigger than ours.
    
    
      
  
      
  
      A real search engine is far more than a trie, but its suggestion box starts as one. Routers match an address's longest prefix in a trie of bits; the networks course takes that further.
      

  
      
    
      Check yourself — 8 of 9
      
    
      How many bytes do the five names' 14 nodes take?
      
    
      
      
      
      
    
      
    
      14 × 216 = 3,024. The speed is paid for in memory.
      
  
      

  
      
    
      
      Dig deeper · the memory fix
      
      Keep only the children that exist, in a map. Or join every chain with no branch into one edge: a compressed trie. CSE-2430 goes further.

      Section 9
      Finding a pattern: the suffix trie
      
    ⏱ 6 min
  
      

  
      Segment 2's match(t, p) tries every start k, and its comparison count is an exam favourite. A suffix is a terminal substring: ullah is the suffix of abdullah that starts at INDEX 4.
      

  
      
    
      Your turn
      
    
      P occurs in T wherever some suffix of T begins with P. You have insert and startsWith. How do you find whether ll occurs in abdullah, and where?
      
    
    
      
      
      Insert every suffix of T, then call startsWith("ll"): true. The suffix below that node is llah, of length 4, so the place is INDEX = n − length + 1 = 8 − 4 + 1 = 5.

      Check yourself — 9 of 9
      
    
      In the suffix trie of abdullah, suggest("a") prints abdullah and ah. Where does a occur?

      Dig deeper · many names in one text
      
      
        
      findAll starts a walk at every k, as Segment 2 does, but with the whole trie in place of one P. On abuanasabdullah it finds abu at INDEX 1, anas at 4, and abdul and abdullah at 8, looking at 31 letters. Segment 2's match, run once for each of the five names over every start, makes 94 comparisons.
      
        
      
        
      Aho–Corasick reads the text only once. It is named here only.
      
      
      
    
      
  
      

      



      
  10
  
      
  
      
    
      Section 10
      The page to keep
      
    ⏱ 2 min
  
      
  
      
    
      
    
      
      
      Search and insert
      One walk, one step per letter. They differ in two lines: line 7 (give up, or make the node) and line 11 (read the mark, or set it).
      
      
      Two ways to fail
      No edge for the next letter. Or the walk ends on a node with no mark.
      
      
      Starts with…
      Walk to the prefix's node; everything below it is the answer, printed a to z.
      
      
      Cost
      O(L) time for a word of L letters. 216 bytes a node.
      
      
      The suffix trie
      P occurs in T exactly where a suffix of T begins with P. INDEX = n − length + 1.

        E · about 2 marks
        Compare
        
  
        LikelyNot yet set2 marks
        
    
        Why does an autocomplete box use a trie and not a hash table?
        
  
        A hash table finds one whole word in O(L), but it keeps no order: "everything starting abd" means checking every word. In the trie the prefix is one node, and its subtree comes out in order.
