Section 2

Preorder, inorder, postorder

⏱ 8 min

Lipschutz gives the three walks of a binary tree T with root R as three short recipes:

Preorder (N L R)
  1. Process the root R.
  2. Traverse the left subtree of R in preorder.
  3. Traverse the right subtree of R in preorder.
Inorder (L N R)
  1. Traverse the left subtree of R in inorder.
  2. Process the root R.
  3. Traverse the right subtree of R in inorder.
Postorder (L R N)
  1. Traverse the left subtree of R in postorder.
  2. Traverse the right subtree of R in postorder.
  3. Process the root R.

Preorder writes each node at its first pass, inorder at its second, postorder at its third.

Section 5
The expression tree, by hand

    ⏱ 12 min
  


  
An operator works on two operands. Draw it as a node, with its two operands as its two subtrees. Then a whole expression is a tree.


  

  

    
Your turn

    

      
In (A + B * C) + ((D / E − F) * G), which operator is done last?

    

    
    

      
The middle +. Every other operator is inside a bracket, so each bracket is finished first. The operator done last is the root.

    

  


  

    
The rule, as the exam wants it

    
1. Remove brackets that wrap the whole expression. 2. A single operand is a leaf. 3. Otherwise, of the operators outside all brackets, take the one with the lowest priority; if several tie, take the rightmost. That is the root. 4. Its left subtree is everything to its left, its right subtree everything to its right. Repeat on each side.

What is the root of the tree for A * B + C * D?

Its walks are the Polish forms

    ⏱ 8 min
  


  
Walk the expression tree from Section 5:

Check yourself — 3 of 4

    
Which walk of an expression tree gives its postfix (reverse Polish) form?

    

      
      
      
    

    
Postorder. Each operator is written after both of its operands, which is what postfix means. Preorder gives prefix.

Section 7
The machine method

    ⏱ 6 min
  


  
A program does not hunt for "the operator done last". It uses what you built in Segment 4. First convert to postfix. Then read the postfix left to right with one stack:

  
    
    
  • An operand: push it as a leaf.
    • 
    
  • An operator: pop a subtree, pop another, join them under the operator, and push the result.
    • 
  


  

    
Your turn

    

      
An operator pops two subtrees. Which one comes off the stack first: its left operand or its right?

    

    
    

      
The right one. It was pushed last, so it is on top.

Section 8
Traps

    ⏱ 4 min
  

  
    
    
  • Hidden multiplication. 3m means 3 * m, and (3m + n)(5x − y)³ has a * between the brackets. Autumn 2022 set exactly this one; pick it in the Section 5 simulator.
    • 
    
  • The power is done first. ^ has the highest priority. Chained powers, A ^ B ^ C, follow Segment 4's rule, read left to right. No exam has chained them.
    • 
    
  • Equal priority. In A − B + C the root is the +: of equal operators, the rightmost is done last.
    •

Section 9
The page to keep

    ⏱ 4 min
  

  

    

      
The three walks

      
    
        
  • Preorder N L R: root first.
    • 
        
  • Inorder L N R: root in the middle.
    • 
        
  • Postorder L R N: root last.
    • 
        
  • One walk round the tree: 1st, 2nd, 3rd pass.
    • 
      

    

    

      
The code

      
    
        
  • If the tree is empty, return.
    • 
        
  • Otherwise print the root and walk both subtrees; where the print goes decides the order.
    • 
        
  • n nodes means 2n + 1 calls.
    • 
      

    

    

      
Expression trees

      
    
        
  • Root = the operator done last: lowest priority outside brackets, rightmost of equals.
    • 
        
  • Preorder = prefix, postorder = postfix, inorder loses the brackets.
    • 
        
  • By machine: postfix, then one stack.
    •

Practice
From the book

  
Lipschutz Example 7.7. E = [a + (b − c)] * [(d − e) / (f + g − h)]: find the preorder and postorder.

  
?Show the answer
    
Preorder * + a − b c / − d e − + f g h. Postorder a b c − + d e − f g + h − / *. Note f + g − h: equal priority, so the − is the root of that part.

  

  
Supplementary 7.24. Draw the trees of (a − 3b)(2x − y)³ and (2a + 5b)³(x − 7y)⁴.

  
?Show the answer
    
First: preorder * − a * 3 b ^ − * 2 x y 3, postorder a 3 b * − 2 x * y − 3 ^ *.

    
Second: preorder * ^ + * 2 a * 5 b 3 ^ − x * 7 y 4, postorder 2 a * 5 b * + 3 ^ x 7 y * − 4 ^ *.
