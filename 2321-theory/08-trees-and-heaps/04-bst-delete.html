Your turn

13 has two children, so its parent cannot simply skip it. Some key must move into 13's place, and the BST rule must still hold. Which keys could do it?

11, the largest key on 13's left, or 15, the smallest key on its right. Either one is bigger than everything left of it and smaller than everything right of it. Lipschutz uses 15, the key just after 13 in inorder: the inorder successor.

Section 2

The three cases

⏱ 16 min

What happens depends only on how many children the node has. These are Lipschutz's words (§7.9):

Case 1. N has no children. Then N is deleted from T by simply replacing the location of N in the parent node P(N) by the null pointer.

Case 2. N has exactly one child. Then N is deleted from T by simply replacing the location of N in P(N) by the location of the only child of N.

Case 3. N has two children. Let S(N) denote the inorder successor of N. Then N is deleted from T by first deleting S(N) from T (by using Case 1 or Case 2) and then replacing node N in T by the node S(N).

The deck deletes four keys, one after another, from the tree in Section 1. Each tab starts from the tree the last one left.

Simulator Four deletions, one after another
n b p r 1 / 1
1

Your turn

To find 13's successor you went one step right, then left as far as you could. Why can the successor never have a left child?

A left child of the successor would be smaller than it and still bigger than 13. That child would come right after 13 in inorder, so it would be the successor. That is why deleting the successor is always case 1 or case 2.

Section 3

The code

⏱ 10 min

The delete is recursive. del(t, item) deletes item from the subtree whose root is t, and returns the root that subtree has now. The caller stores what comes back in its own link. Usually the same node comes back. When a node is taken out, its child (or NULL) comes back instead.

Check yourself — 4 of 4

    
A tree has preorder X Y and postorder Y X. Do these two walks fix the tree?

    

      
      
    

    
No. X is the root and Y its child, but Y could be the left child or the right child. Only an inorder (Y X or X Y) would say which.

Section 7
Back to the BST

    ⏱ 4 min
  

  
A BST's inorder is free: it is its keys, sorted. So a preorder alone fixes a BST. Pair it with the sorted keys and rebuild, exactly as in Section 5. The fourth tab does this with Spring 2025's preorder 40 20 10 30 70 60 90, and gets the same tree Trees 3 built by inserting.

  
That is why Trees 3's "build a BST from its preorder" always has exactly one answer.

Section 8
The page to keep

    ⏱ 2 min
  

  

    

      
The three cases

      
    
        
  • No child: the parent's link becomes NULL.
    • 
        
  • One child: the parent's link points to that child.
    • 
        
  • Two children: delete the inorder successor (case 1 or 2), then put it in the node's place.
    • 
      

    

    

      
The successor

      
    
        
  • One step right, then left as far as possible.
    • 
        
  • It never has a left child.
    • 
        
  • The root is no different.
    • 
      

    

    

      
Two walks, one tree

      
    
        
  • Preorder's first key (or postorder's last) is the root.
    • 
        
  • It splits the inorder into left and right. Repeat.
    • 
        
  • Without an inorder, no; for a BST, the inorder is the sorted keys.
    • 
      

    

  


  

    
Next class

    
A different tree, with a different rule: the heap.

    
A heap keeps the largest key at the root and stays complete, so it fits in an array with no gaps. Segment 5's priority queue paid O(n) to find the largest; a heap pays O(log n).

Question and answer
"How do you delete a node?", and the m-way tree

  

    
FinalSpring 2024 · Q4 (b)5 marks

    
Write the difference between binary search tree and m-way search tree. How do you delete a node from the binary search tree? Explain the deletion with example.

  

  
    
    
  • A BST node holds one key and at most two children. An m-way search tree node holds up to m − 1 keys, in order, and up to m children; the keys between two children separate their subtrees.
    • 
    
  • With more keys per node, an m-way tree is shorter, so a search visits fewer nodes. That matters when each node is a disk block. CSE-2421 teaches B-trees, the balanced m-way trees.
    • 
    
  • For the deletion half, write the three cases above, with the deck's example: delete 4 (case 1), 10 (case 2), then 13 (case 3, its successor 15 takes its place).
