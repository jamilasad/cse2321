Section 1

What Segment 5's queue paid

⏱ 6 min

A priority queue serves the most important item first, not the one that came first. Segment 5 built it two ways: as a sorted one-way list, and as a 2-D array with one queue per priority.

Your turn

Which operation is slow in each layout: adding an item, or taking out the most important one?

The sorted list pays on insert: the new item walks along the list to its place, O(n). Taking the first item is O(1).

The 2-D array pays on delete: it scans for the first row that is not empty, O(P) for P priorities. Adding to the end of a row is O(1).

A heap makes both operations cost the depth of a tree. That is about log₂ n steps: 20 steps for a million items. The rest of this class is how.

Section 2

The heap

⏱ 11 min

You already know a complete tree: every level is full except perhaps the last, and the last fills from the left. A heap is a complete tree with one more rule.

Definition · Lipschutz 7.55

A heap (or maxheap) is a complete binary tree H in which every node is greater than or equal to each of its children. A minheap is the mirror: every node is less than or equal to each of its children.

So the largest key is always at the root. A heap needs no pointers. Trees 1 stored a tree in an array: the children of TREE[K] are TREE[2K] and TREE[2K + 1]. That tree had an empty cell, because it was not complete. A heap is complete, so its n keys fill TREE[1] to TREE[n] with no gap.

Your turn

Spring 2026 gave this array of a max heap: [90, 70, 80, 40, 60, 10, 50]. Draw it as a tree.

90 is the root. Its children are TREE[2] = 70 and TREE[3] = 80. 70's children are TREE[4] = 40 and TREE[5] = 60; 80's are TREE[6] = 10 and TREE[7] = 50. As text: 90(70(40, 60), 80(10, 50)). Every parent is bigger than its children, so it is a heap.

Complete tree or heap? Every heap is complete. A complete tree need not be a heap. Pick a picture, then click a box to see its parent and its children.

The birds are Trees 1's family, but here their colours, crests and flowers mean nothing. A bigger key is a bigger bird, and the plate under each door gives the key.

Explorer Heap, or not?
i

Do not mix a heap up with a BST. Here are the same seven keys in both.

A BST orders keys left to right: smaller on the left, larger on the right. A heap orders them top to bottom: a parent is bigger than both children, and its two children have no order between them.

TREE[4], because ⌊9/2⌋ = 4. TREE[18] is 9's left child. TREE[5] is 4's other child, 9's uncle.

Check yourself — 2 of 4
In a tree, every parent is at least as big as its children. But its last level has a gap on the left. Is it a heap?
No. A heap must be complete as well as ordered. With a gap, its keys cannot fill TREE[1] to TREE[n], and the rule 2K, 2K + 1 points at an empty cell.

When the root of a heap is deleted, which key moves into TREE[1] first?

    

      
      
    

    
The last key. Taking it keeps the tree complete: the row still runs 1 to n − 1 with no gap. It then sinks. Moving the larger child up leaves the gap somewhere in the middle.

Section 7
What it costs

    ⏱ 4 min
  


  

    
    

      
An insert climbs one path, and a delete sinks down one path. Neither can be longer than the tree is deep.

      
A complete tree doubles at every level. So n keys need only ⌊log₂ n⌋ + 1 levels. A million keys fit in 20 levels, because 2²⁰ − 1 = 1,048,575. Lipschutz prints 21 (7.4); it is 20.

    

  


  

    
Insert
O(log n)

    
Delete the root
O(log n)

    
Each walks one path from a leaf to the root, or back: at most c × log₂ n steps. Building a heap by inserting n keys therefore costs at most n × log₂ n: O(n log n).

  


  
That keeps Section 1's promise:

Section 8
The page to keep

    ⏱ 2 min
  

  

    

      
The heap

      
    
        
  • A complete tree where every parent ≥ its children.
    • 
        
  • Stored in TREE[1..n], no gaps. Children of K: 2K and 2K + 1. Parent: ⌊K/2⌋.
    • 
        
  • Every heap is complete; a complete tree need not be a heap.
    • 
      

    

    

      
Insert, and build

      
    
        
  • Put the key in TREE[n + 1].
    • 
        
  • While it is bigger than its parent, they exchange.
    • 
        
  • Build: insert the keys one at a time; draw the heap after each.
    • 
      

    

    

      
Delete the root

      
    
        
  • The root leaves; the last key moves to TREE[1].
    • 
        
  • It sinks, exchanging with its larger child.
    • 
        
  • Both cost the depth: O(log n).
    •

Delete the root again and again, and the keys come out largest first.

    
That is heapsort. Next class also builds the priority queue on a heap, and finds the k largest of a long list with a small heap.

Questions and answers
What is a max heap? Build one

  
The definition. A max heap is a complete binary tree in which every node is greater than or equal to each of its children. So the largest key is at the root. It is stored in an array: the children of TREE[K] are TREE[2K] and TREE[2K + 1].

  
The method. Insert the keys one at a time, in the order given. Each new key goes into the next free place, then exchanges with its parent while it is bigger. Draw the heap after every insert.

  






  
Question and answer
Complete binary tree against heap tree

  

    
FinalAutumn 2025 · Q4 (a) · Spring 2024 · Q4 (a)2 marks

    
What is the difference between a complete binary tree and a heap tree? Explain with figures.

  

  
    
    
  • A complete binary tree has every level full except perhaps the last, which is filled from the left. It is about shape only: any keys, in any order.
    • 
    
  • A heap tree is a complete binary tree with an order as well: every parent is greater than or equal to its children (a max heap). So its largest key is at the root.
    • 
    
  • Every heap is complete. A complete tree is a heap only if every parent is big enough.
    •

FinalSpring 2026 · Q4 (c)2 marks

    
Consider the following array representation of a Max-Heap: [90, 70, 80, 40, 60, 10, 50]. Draw the tree representation of this heap.
